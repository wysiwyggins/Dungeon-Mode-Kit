# More Patterns

Hello from Montreal, my new home for at least the next four years.

The architect function that came out of the last design cycle, `applyTilePattern`, is complicated and had a single purpose—duplicating the old `addBaseAndShadows` background tile placements. Before I could move on to more important tasks I wanted to make sure I used it for at least another case, so this push adds some patterned entity placement for decorating biomes. It's a stub for more interesting uses later I hope. Or maybe this is enough!

I'm reading ahead a little before my first class starts: Dearden. 2006. *Designing as a conversation with digital materials*. My goal is to get the complex expensive guts of this thing done in a way that makes them non-specific, reusable tools so that I can focus on the recombinatoric play of level and prototype design. I'm self conscious about decisions I've made at previous stages and I want to do better going forward, and that is going to mean revisiting and processing all the stuff that's currently in engine and architect (don't even get me started on effects.js...)

One of my notes from this reading:

> Design as a conversation with the materials themselves, which exhibit differing degrees of resistance. Imagine drawing on paper, the will to a line is resisted by paper grain and the behavior of graphite. The material utterance is the result, which I infer distinguishes it from the (gesture?) of carving, drawing, etc. or the intent/idea
> Applied to software we might think of the difficulty of coding, the complexity of a system as it scales, performance considerations of hardware. Vibe coding removes some of these considerations in a way that can make the result more homogenized and less distinctive, while increasing the secondary considerations of cognitive friction and complexity, both in the designer who is alienated from the resulting design utterance and even in the llm system which has to re-orient itself to the system over and over based on documentation and filling a context window with a re-investigation of the code. There is never the architectural knowledge that comes from ‘inhabiting’ a codebase other than at a high level.
